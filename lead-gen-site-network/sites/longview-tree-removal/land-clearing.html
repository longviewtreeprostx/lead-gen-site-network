An overgrown lot or unmarked tree line doesn't just look bad — it can hold up a construction timeline, hide a drainage problem, or eat into pasture you're paying property taxes on either way. We clear land across Longview, Kilgore, White Oak, Gladewater, and Hallsville for homeowners, builders, and landowners who need it done completely, not just knocked back for a season.

What we clear

New construction & building lots

Full clearing ahead of a home build, driveway, or outbuilding — trees, stumps, brush, and debris removed and hauled or mulched so your builder or excavator has a clean site to start from. We can coordinate timing directly with your builder if there's a schedule to hit.

Pasture & agricultural clearing

Opening up overgrown pasture, clearing fence lines, or removing invasive brush and volunteer trees that have taken over grazing land. We can leave larger shade trees standing on request instead of clearing wall to wall.

Brush & underbrush clearing

Thick underbrush, vines, and small trees cleared from a property line, a wooded lot you want walkable again, or an area that's become a fire or pest concern. Selective clearing is an option if you want the canopy kept and just the understory cleaned out.

Equipment we use

Most jobs are handled with a forestry mulcher for brush and small-diameter trees, which grinds material on-site instead of leaving a burn pile or a truckload of debris to haul off. Larger trees are cut and removed the same way as a standalone tree removal job. For sites where the ground needs to be pushed and graded afterward, we can bring in a skid steer or coordinate with an excavation contractor — just let us know what the finished site needs to look like and we'll scope it accordingly.

Permits

Land clearing permit requirements vary by whether you're inside Longview city limits or in unincorporated Gregg/Upshur County, and by lot size and intended use. We'll tell you upfront if your project is likely to need a permit, but you (or your builder, if this is part of a construction project) are responsible for confirming with the local permitting office before work starts — this is standard for land clearing anywhere in Texas, not something specific to us.

From first cut to finished site

Land clearing isn't just about taking trees and brush down — what the site looks like when we leave matters just as much. For construction and building-lot work, that usually means a clean, grade-ready surface with trunks, brush, and debris hauled off or mulched, so your builder or excavation crew isn't working around leftover material. For pasture or acreage clearing, we can leave everything mulched in place instead of hauling it out — the ground-up material breaks down and adds organic matter back into the soil, which many landowners prefer over hauling material off unnecessarily. Tell us the end use for the property — new construction, pasture, a walkable wooded lot — and we'll clear and finish it to match.

When to schedule land clearing

Late fall through winter is generally the easiest season to clear land in East Texas — underbrush has died back, ticks and poison ivy are dormant, and the ground is typically firmer for equipment than after a wet spring. That said, clearing ahead of new construction usually runs on the builder's timeline rather than the calendar, and we can mobilize outside the ideal season when a project schedule requires it. If you're clearing pasture or a wooded lot with no hard deadline, we're glad to advise on timing that makes the job easier and less costly.

Frequently asked questions

How much does land clearing cost?

Cost depends mainly on acreage, how dense the vegetation is, whether large trees need to come down, and what the finished site needs to look like (mulched in place vs. fully cleared and hauled). We'll walk the property and give you a firm price before starting — see our pricing & cost guide for general ranges on related services.

Can you leave some trees standing?

Yes — selective clearing is common, especially for pasture work where landowners want to keep shade trees, or residential lots where a few established trees are worth preserving. Tell us what you want kept before we start, and we'll clear around it.

What happens to the cleared material?

Your choice — we can mulch brush and smaller material on-site (useful ground cover or a future soil amendment) or haul everything away, including larger trunks. Let us know your preference when we scope the job.
